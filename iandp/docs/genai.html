French Puppet Show, Mary Browne, Diary of a Girl in France in 1821

step 2: basic research & overview

This is the work of Mary Browne, who catalogued her travel to France with family in 1821. Mary was 14 when she drew these images, and generally held a negative and unimpressed view of France. Her illustrations serve somewhat as a historical account of culture, capturing all the events and people that stood out to her.
This diary serves a viewing of France through Mary Browne's eyes. The public domain review quotes that "Little is known about Mary's short life (she died when just 26 years old). The introduction (of the diary), written by her niece Euphemia Stewart Browne, tells of how she was a keen naturalist and later turned her artistic talents to painting "an exquisite collection of butterflies and moths" which was left unfinished at her untimely death."

step 3: What interests me

style
The drawings, though illustrative, are almost scientific study. People watching put down as sketches.
motivation
Although Browne seemed apathetic to her experiences in France, the care in the drawings reflects more curiosity and interest than her writing would suggest.
story quality
There is some charm to these images, like they accompany a novel still unfolding.
composition
The central composition is consistent through the images without being boring.

The round top has a collection of miniature figures, including a horse and its rides, a man behind them in a top hat, a small figure in a similar outfit to the second woman in the image and another figure walking behind them.

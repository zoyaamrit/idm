Time Capsule

Desc
design a time capsule, looking closely at the things you live with and hold onto
When
Oct - Nov 26

Part 1: The Collection

For this time capsule, I chose to concentrate my collection to jewelry. Of all the objects I own, the most discernible collections are my books and my earrings. For a capsule, I think my jewelry fits all the possible paramters: it is worn, precious, ordinary, inherited, strange, worn out, sometimes-forgotten, connected to people, place, and routine.

PEARL FLOWER
These earrings have been essentially ever present, I feel like I've seen them worn for years by my mom. She still has another set very similar to this, one she wears more often that I do this.
JHUMKA
Green in an unconventional colour for a Jhumka, the beads are normally white and more often contrasted with gold. Also from my mom's collection, this was one earring that my sister stole and wore nearly daily. The colour clashing with the red of her school uniform, until I kept it. Now it's seen with my most regular wear, jeans and a plain shirt, the only colour the bright green of the earring.
BLUE GRAPES
Bought in 2024 at home, this was an earring I picked out with my mom. It is close to a Jhumka but contains the core of it, just the beads, no cover. It is perhaps, an extension of inheritance. With each iteration, each generation, less is shrouded.
DANGLING
A complete depature from the style of everything in the collection so far, perhaps all it has in common with the other is its length and its sway. It is different in colour, in where it came from (the US) and who has worn it. This is a piece I acquired alone, without history but in tandem with it.

Part 2: The Capsule

While designing the capsule, my primary concern was the presentation of the objects. I wanted to document the earrings in a way that captured their physicality, the way the move, and the sounds they make. Another thing that I felt was important to include was the context of the objects, and why they are important to me. To capture this simulatenously, I decided to make a box that frames the objects and also gives me a way to put down in writing their context.

To achieve this, I layered two photo frames with a glass pane in between. The earrings are suspended on wires across each frame, divided by whether I inherited them or acquired them myself. The glass pane allows me to write on it, and the frames allow me to put down the context of each earring. The frames also present the earrings with some prestige, and some drama, giving them a sense of importance and value.


Note on the Frame

Half of these objects are inherited, half acquired. Jewelery has always had value for me, culturally, aesthetically, and sentimentally. It is a way to be grown up. To build my own taste and identity but somehow I always come back to all the thing my parents have given me, the jhumkas, the rings, my taste, my personality, my childhood. It's odd and wonderful to find yourself invariably and inevitably linked to the people and places you come from.

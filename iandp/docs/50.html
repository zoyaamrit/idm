Ideation & Prototyping 50 Ways of Seeing

50 ways of seeing

Desc
imagine and interpret 50 ways of seeing a mug
When
Sept 26
Medium
so many

I chose the most ordinary mug I could find. It is plain, glazed white and has a single loop handle. A basic view of it will reveal nothing, no chips or indents or story attached. I wanted to begin with something emptier, so that I could extend the form further.


The first thing I did was to sketch the mug as I saw it, and then begin adding variations to it. I did a blind contour drawing and instantly lost the precise form but the meaning was still present. Moving from pen to pencil to marker, the legibility of the object as a mug was never lost. Once I switched over to paint, it became a more interesting exercise.


Using paint automatically attached some play with shadow and light to the exercise. I was automatically adding layering, playing with color and trying to work more gesturally. The instinctual switch was not something I expected to happen, especially as I tried to limit myself first to the color palette of the mug itself (whites and shadows). This made me think about playing with shadows, and I switched over to a digital space and used the oil pastel tools to try and add more color to those shadows. The layering made me consider watercolor which instantly became my next medium. With this material I lost some amount of control due to my unfamiliarity with watercolor. I tried to embrace the looseness of the water’s movement and ended up with a slightly wonky pink mug. The way the colors were stacking really made me consider transparency, and I wanted to try making the mug in layer. First the ‘back’ of it, then its contents and then the front. I ended up with a slightly glassy looking mug than the solid ceramic material that my observed object was.


Drawing the content of the mug made me consider the object more metaphorically. How else can a mug be represented? I thought about the materiality of what a mug contains, and represented the mug as memory. A small catalog of everything it has contained, water, tea, coffee, chocolate, smoothies, lemonade. The mug then becomes a material of memory. I then thought about the purpose of the mug, to contain and its most nascent form would simply be cupped hands. Do cupped hands signal a mug? Or somewhere for liquid to sit?


The relationship between hands and the mug also became interesting to me, so I wrapped the mug with plastic and made records of how I hold it: by the handle, in one hand or cupped in two. This was probably the most cumbersome material and process I used. I ended up with imprints of hands, abstracted visuals that were made from a mug but look nothing like it.


From here I started playing more with materials. Pulling things out from the kitchen, I adopted the shape of the mug in foil, with cardboard, with parchment paper etc. The most interesting (and extremely complicated) one was where I attempted to make a mug out of coffee, switching its contents around. I used instant coffee and flour as a base for clay and then tried to shape them into a mug. I want to explore this further, and try to invert the purpose of something, coffee as container rather than contained.


Working with cardboard and paper made me curious about technical ways of seeing, so I made a blueprint, orthographic projection and a cross section of the mug. Working out its dimensions and proportions made me realize how much of a mug is logistics. I began looking up how mugs are made and reproduced the process of throwing one in stop motion.


Once I began animating, a format I am comfortable with, it became easier to abstract shapes. I began drawing the mug as circles and negative space, considering its construction in purely graphic form. In a short animation, I constructed the mug by dissecting a circle and then arranging its parts as a container. This graphic representation made me considering branding and how a typographic identity of the mug would exist. I attempted two versions of this representation, using the letters M U and G to create the same visual shape of a mug.


I switched over to using technology at this point. I photographed the mug in different ways, using a wide lens to distort its shape and capture its interior. Pixelating the image was a brief experiment but it did not abstract the image in the way I was imagining it would. I considered also the use of the mug, and tried to the capture the sound of it. I poured myself a drink and recorded all the sounds made it the process, and then a shortened version of just the mug and my interactions with it. The sound of a mug hitting the counter, a spoon moving in it or tapping the rim is distinct and surprisingly recognizable.


I think this exploration was successful, and I enjoyed moving through it iteratively. It is something I normally struggle with, but given the tight parameters of my inspiration, I was forced to follow every small thought I had.
